# LLM-chatbot v2: design

Status: revision 4 for Alexander's approval, 2026-11-01, after four independent reviews (see
"Review log"). Build follows the delivery tiers below: the minimum credible version first. Extends `docs/V2_PLAN.md`; where they differ, this document wins and the plan gets
updated to match.

## Intent

The product's core promise, unchanged since v1: **the reader can verify every claim against the
source at the same time as reading the answer.** v2 makes that promise much stronger (every claim
tied to a quoted, highlighted region of the source, checked for support before it is shown) and
measures it with checks that do not grade their own work.

v2 is the strongest manual-QA system we can build with models we can obtain and run, proven by an
evaluation a hiring engineer at an AI company would trust: a held-out test set with one pre-registered
headline number, a manual published after the models' training cutoffs, a test of whether the system
follows the manual when it contradicts the model's beliefs, recognised public benchmarks under a
stated protocol, and human checks of gold answers and grading. Every component is chosen by
measurement and every rejected candidate is recorded with the reason. Every model sits behind a
standard API, so the system runs on one GPU for development and scales to real inference servers
without code changes.

## What the v1 baseline told us

From `evals/results/NOTES.md` (v1, 42 questions, 3 repeats):

- Retrieval is mostly fine (recall@5 0.89, candidate recall 0.94); reading is not (correctness 0.49).
- Tables flattened by text extraction wreck answers (parts manual correctness 0.03; one answer gave a
  CAGE code as a part number: right row, wrong column).
- Figures are retrieved but unreadable (figure correctness 0.20, recall@5 1.00).
- The 0.35 score gate drops correct pages; v1 also answers questions the manual does not cover.

So v2's centre of gravity is reading and verifiability: OCR-grade parsing, structured tables, page
images for a vision model, claim-level citations checked deterministically, and abstention from
evidence.

## Goals

1. **Verifiable at a glance (the core promise).** Every claim shown is tied to a verbatim quote from a
   specific source region (text line, table row with its header, or figure), highlighted beside the
   answer. A claim is shown only once its quote has matched the source (milliseconds); claims that then
   fail the remaining checks are withdrawn into a "claim removed, see why" disclosure. Figure claims
   follow their own path (see Query) and are labelled as read from a figure.
2. Measurably better answers than v1 on one pre-registered headline metric, with gains attributed
   through ablations and reported with the smallest difference the eval can detect.
3. Figures and tables answered correctly; table rows shown with their headers, figures cropped from
   the source.
4. Abstention decided from evidence, reported as a risk-coverage curve and one pre-chosen operating point.
5. Multi-turn chat with streamed answers.
6. A tool-using answer loop (search, table lookup, page reading) scored on a small agent task set.
7. A decision record for every component, including what lost and why.
8. Credible numbers (see Evaluation).
9. Runs against any OpenAI-compatible endpoint; a fully open-weights configuration that fits one
   16 GB GPU (the laptop 4090) and that a stranger can reproduce; the 5090 is only for optional larger
   rows.
10. **Demo-ready at the end:** v1's interface concept, redesigned (see Demo stage).

## Non-goals

- Production auth, multi-tenancy, or hosting a public demo (demo-ready means it runs on demand for a
  walkthrough, with a replay mode).
- Training or fine-tuning models.
- Non-PDF inputs.
- Leaderboard comparisons. Public benchmarks are run under our protocol and never placed next to
  published leaderboard numbers.

## Anti-goals (failure even if it works)

- An answer whose claims the reader cannot check against the source in one glance.
- A citation that points at a real source which does not support the claim, shown as if it did.
- So many flags that readers stop trusting answers (false-flag budget below).
- Numbers that no committed results file backs, that come from tuning on reported questions, or that
  are inflated by memorisation or by a grader favouring its own family.
- A pipeline that only runs on Alexander's hardware or needs undocumented services.
- Any change to the Sparks' services, or load that degrades household use (OpenWebUI, Home Assistant
  voice).
- Secrets, LAN addresses or host names in the public repo.

## Constraints

- Public repo, MIT licence unless a licence decision says otherwise (see Licensing).
- Endpoints, keys and host names in `.env` only. No paid APIs.
- Hardware: laptop RTX 4090 16 GB (WSL, x86; parsers, embedder, reranker, stores, eval), optionally
  a 5090 32 GB, and the Sparks' production Qwen3.8 through the existing LiteLLM gateway (no new
  services on the Sparks).
- Human time: Alexander's checks total about 4–6 hours, in sessions of 30–45 minutes (see Human checks).

## Approaches considered

| | Approach | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| A | Text-first: parser → structured text; figures described at ingestion; text retrieval only. | Cheap; capped by parser and caption quality. |
| B | Image-first: pages retrieved by a page-image retriever and read by a vision model. | Lossless on visuals; weak on exact identifiers; heavy index. |
| C | Both fused: text and page-image retrieval in parallel. | Scales to large visual corpora; most moving parts. |
| D | Text retrieval picks pages; their page images go to the vision generator with the text. | Most of C's reading benefit with one index; misses pages text search cannot find. |

**Chosen: D first, then C (time-boxed to 2 days), built modular.** Alexander chose C as the target;
review 1 showed that on 360 pages retrieval already finds the page 94% of the time, so D delivers the
first gain with one index. The page-image index follows because it is what scales, and the ablation
reports what it adds here. Each half can be switched off by configuration.

## Architecture

Every model-backed component is reached over a standard HTTP API, so placement is configuration only:

| Component | API | Default placement |
|---|---|---|
| Generator, vision model | OpenAI `/v1/chat/completions` (images as `image_url`) | Qwen3.8 via LiteLLM; laptop models for open-weights rows |
| Judges | same | see Evaluation |
| Text embedder | `/v1/embeddings` | laptop (vLLM or in-process) |
| Reranker | `/v1/rerank` | laptop |
| Page-image retriever (step C) | embeddings or in-process | laptop / 5090 |
| Parser and OCR | in-process | laptop |

**Code:** plain Python modules with small interfaces (`Parser`, `Embedder`, `Reranker`,
`PageRetriever`, `Generator`, `SupportChecker`, `TableStore`), no orchestration framework. Every
stage records its inputs and outputs for the eval harness. FastAPI backend with server-sent events.

### Ingestion

1. **Parse** each PDF into blocks with page numbers and boxes: text lines, headings, tables (cells
   intact; cell or row boxes from a table-cell detector where the layout parser gives only table
   boxes), figures (crop, box, caption). Block coordinates are a hard requirement for a parser.
2. **Scanned pages are always OCR'd;** an embedded text layer is ignored when it is malformed (for
   example letter-spaced). Detection is automatic and logged.
3. **One coordinate system:** every box is stored in page-normalised [0,1] coordinates, measured on
   the same rendered image used for display and for the vision generator (origin, CropBox/MediaBox and
   rotation handled once, in one function, with tests).
4. **Chunk by structure:** sections and procedures whole up to a size limit; tables as their own chunks
   with caption and header repeated.
5. **Tables to a structured store:** every table goes into SQLite with headers, source page and row
   boxes. A `lookup_table` tool finds rows exactly (for example figure 15, item 17 → part number) and
   returns the row with its header for citation. A general tool for any manual, not a parts special case.
6. **Figures:** a vision model writes a description, and for charts and plates a data table, indexed
   as text and linked to the crop.
7. **Pages:** rendered at 150 dpi for display; downscaled (about 1,000 px long edge) for the generator.
   In step C also embedded by the page-image retriever.
8. **Index** in Qdrant (local mode for development, server for deployment): dense and sparse (BM25)
   text vectors; multi-vector page embeddings in step C.

### Query

1. Follow-up turns are rewritten into standalone questions using chat history.
2. Text retrieval: dense + sparse, fused (RRF), reranked over a wide pool; MMR, if kept, selects from
   the reranked pool. In step C page-image hits are fused at page level; a page-image hit with no text
   chunk is passed as a page image only.
3. Table lookups when the question names identifiers, or when the generator calls `lookup_table`.
4. The generator gets text excerpts with block ids, at most about 3 page images, and table rows. It
   answers in a line-delimited claim format (one tagged line per claim: claim text, block id, verbatim
   quote) that can be parsed while streaming. Reasoning mode is off unless measured to help.
5. **Support check per claim, in order:**
   1. *Quote match:* text normalised (whitespace, hyphenation, ligatures, case, common OCR confusions)
      and aligned to the block's text with fuzzy partial matching; the match maps to OCR lines, which
      gives the highlight boxes. No match → the claim fails. *Figure claims* (cited to a figure or a
      page image rather than a text block) skip quote matching: they are checked by entailment against
      the figure's description and data table, shown with the figure crop, and labelled "read from
      figure N".
   2. *Deterministic facts:* every number and identifier in the claim must appear in the quote; for
      table claims it must sit in the column whose header matches the claim's attribute, checked with
      the row and its header together. This catches right-row-wrong-column errors.
   3. *Entailment* for what remains: a small NLI model or LLM check of claim against quote with header.
   Unknown block ids are dropped and counted.
6. **Display states:** verified (quiet highlight); read from a page image (neutral icon, figure crop or
   page region); failed (removed from the answer, listed under "N claims removed, see why"). The
   failure thresholds are calibrated to a false-flag budget below 5% on dev.
7. **Abstention:** if no claim survives, the system says it could not find the answer in the manual
   and shows what it found. The continuous score for the risk-coverage curve is the highest support
   score among the answer's claims (entailment probability; a deterministic pass counts as 1.0). A
   retrieval-score threshold is only a fallback.
8. **Streaming:** each claim is held until it closes and its quote matches (milliseconds), then shown
   with its highlight and a neutral "checking" mark; deterministic and entailment checks run
   asynchronously in batches and settle the state (a claim that fails is withdrawn into the
   disclosure). Time to first claim is measured.

**Generator-specific formats:** Command R7B is text-only; it runs with its native grounded-generation
citation format, converted to the claim structure, and its rows are labelled text-only.

### Agent (folded into the answer loop)

The pre-registered v2 system is the fixed pipeline in Query plus `lookup_table`. The agent is the
same generator in a tool loop (`search_manuals`, `lookup_table`, `read_page` with text and image,
`ask_user`), reported as its own row. `web_search` is not used: a web claim cannot be highlighted in
the manual, which breaks the core promise. Native tool calling, one loop. Scored on 20
tasks with checkable outcomes (a final value, a declined answer, a clarifying question), reported with
the caveat that 20 tasks only show large effects. Table-of-contents navigation (PageIndex style) is a
stretch experiment.

### Demo stage (final)

Built on v1's GUI stack (React + Vite, `react-pdf` for the document) and its core idea, the answer
beside the live source, redesigned freely (Alexander, 2026-11-01). Highlights are drawn as an
overlay from stored boxes, not from the PDF text layer. The core-promise UI is built in the thin slice;
this stage polishes it and adds:

- click or hover a claim to move the source panel to its evidence (no continuous scroll-sync);
- table answers show the source row with its header inline;
- multi-turn chat, streaming, inline figure crops;
- a "how it found this" panel: retrieved blocks and pages, text vs page-image origin, scores,
  check results;
- one-command startup (Docker Compose and a local no-Docker mode) and a **replay mode** that plays
  recorded runs for interviews without the home network;
- **nice to have, last:** generated diagrams as code (Mermaid flowcharts, chart specs from table
  data), labelled "generated from pages X–Y, not in the manual", hand-checked on a small task set. No
  image-generation models for technical content.

**Demo script (10 minutes):** the parts lookup with the CAGE-code trap; the rating-plate figure; the
Cessna 172S near miss declining and showing what it found; a follow-up turn; the "how it found this"
panel; the per-item eval browser.

## Evaluation

The detailed method lives in `EVAL.md`; the README shows the headline table only.

### Item pool and splits

- One item pool written with one documented procedure, split into **dev and test by manual section**
  (so tuning cannot carry over through shared pages), stratified by category, after writing and before
  any v2 run on test sections. The existing 42 items are dev already (the thin slice runs on them).
- **Dev ≥ 80 items, ≥ 20 unanswerable; test ≥ 200 items**, including:
  - hand-written single-page, multi-page, figure and table items;
  - 30+ near-miss unanswerables (on topic but not covered);
  - templated, mechanically checkable table items, reported as their own sub-score with each template
    counted as one unit;
  - paraphrased, user-style versions of a sample, scored as a robustness delta, not as extra items.
- Provenance recorded per item (written by Claude, edited by Alexander, paraphrased by which model).
- **Held-out fourth manual,** published after the generators' training cutoffs, never used in
  development, with its own near-miss unanswerables; scored only at the end.

### Grading

- Short factual answers by normalised exact match.
- Free-form answers against a **key-fact checklist** per reference answer, not a holistic grade.
- **Primary judge from a different model family than every compared generator** (Qwen and Cohere are
  generators, so neither judges the headline); results shown per judge as well. Length bias checked by
  regressing judge scores on answer length. No pairwise judging.
- Judge validity: agreement with Alexander's blind grades, reported per system (a judge biased on one
  system invalidates comparisons, not just levels).
- A Claude subagent cross-check on a sample remains as an extra, labelled as such.

### Citation metrics (independent of the runtime checker)

- Scored by the independent judge, never by the answer-time checker.
- Citation recall (claims supported by their citations) and citation precision (citations that are
  supportive), per evidence type (text, table row, page image); plus claims per answer, key-fact
  coverage, quote-match rate, removed-claim rate, and the share of whole-page highlights.
- The checker itself is evaluated separately: precision and recall on unsupported claims, from about
  150 labelled claims split by evidence type, including planted negatives (swapped citations, altered
  numbers, right row wrong column).

### Statistics and pre-registration

- **Primary endpoint (binary): the answer is correct, and the independent judge finds its key fact in
  the cited source** (a cited page for v1, which has no claim-level quotes; the cited quote's block for
  v2). Primary contrast: v2 final vs v1. v1 is re-run and re-graded with the new judge; the
  Qwen-graded numbers in `evals/results/NOTES.md` are history, not the comparison. Tested with an exact McNemar test plus a clustered paired bootstrap interval; the
  discordant counts are always printed.
- Resampling unit is the item cluster (template; source item with its paraphrases; page).
  Effective n is reported.
- A small secondary family (for example v2 vs Qwen3.8 alone with whole manuals in context; v2 vs
  closed-book) with Holm correction. All ablations are exploratory: intervals, no significance claims.
- Minimum detectable effect in every table. Rough expectation: about 10 points overall, about 25 points
  per category; stated up front.
- **Pre-registration:** a tagged commit before the test run containing the primary metric and
  contrast, the secondary family, the exploratory list, and the analysis code.
- Repeats for every reported configuration (vLLM at temperature 0 is not fully deterministic), with
  the spread shown.

### Abstention

Risk-coverage curve and its area (AURC), plus one operating point fixed on dev before the test run.

### Knowledge, memorisation and context faithfulness

- **"Answerable without reading" row:** each generator with no manual. A high score mostly shows
  general domain knowledge (likely on the FAA handbook); reported as such, not as leakage.
- **Context faithfulness under knowledge conflict** (the test that matters for a RAG system): about 30
  items whose source facts are counterfactually edited **in the PDF itself** (redact the span, overlay
  the new value in the same font and size with PyMuPDF, re-render, re-parse; verify the parser reads
  the new value and the rendering by eye), so the text path and the page-image path see the same
  edited fact. Every occurrence of the fact is edited and checked by searching the parsed text.
  Raster figures and plates are excluded; any page that cannot be edited cleanly is marked text-path
  only. The same edits for every system.
- Analysis as a difference-in-differences against the "answerable without reading" row on the same
  items; the perturbed score is not assumed to be the honest one.
- The post-cutoff fourth manual is the cleanest memorisation control.

### Public benchmarks (our protocol, separate table)

| Benchmark | Use |
|---|---|
| ViDoRe V3, industrial subset (MTEB, NDCG@10) | Choosing the page-image retriever |
| OmniDocBench, table subset (dataset non-commercial research: evaluate, never redistribute) | Choosing the parser |
| TechManualQA-700 (CC BY 4.0) | End-to-end on unseen manuals, only if the manuals can be obtained legally; our protocol, citation metrics on a hand-labelled subset |

Not used: MMLongBench-Doc (its comparable protocol needs a paid GPT-4o extraction step), Agri-Query
(small, unclear data release). Benchmark results go in their own "our protocol" table with sample IDs
and seeds published, never beside leaderboard numbers; one published baseline model is re-run under
our protocol as an anchor.

### Systems in the final table

v1 as shipped; v1 at temperature 0; answerable-without-reading; v2 with Command R7B (text-only, like
for like); v2 step D; v2 step C; ablations (table store, page images, page-image index, deterministic
check, entailment check); Qwen3.8 alone with whole manuals in context; a long-context router only if
that row wins somewhere.

**Long-context row rules:** same parsed text as v2, same generator as the v2 row it is compared with,
same claim/quote format, prefill tokens and time reported with and without prefix caching; page images
only if they fit.

### Also reported

Failure taxonomy per item (not retrieved, parse error, misread, wrong table row, bad citation, wrong
abstention) and a per-item results browser (artifact); cost per query (tokens, latency p50/p95,
GPU-seconds), ingestion time, index size; pinned model revisions and hashes; raw generations and judge
outputs committed so anyone can re-score without GPUs; the open-weights configuration reported
alongside, and a statement that rows using Qwen3.8 on the Sparks need comparable hardware.

## Human checks (Alexander, about 4–6 hours in 30–45 minute sessions)

Each session is a private artifact built for speed (one item per screen, keyboard shortcuts, source
image alongside). Where possible Alexander confirms or corrects labels I pre-fill; pre-filled labels
are not counted as human until he has confirmed them, and the README states exactly what was checked.

1. Gold answers: about 60 test items, split by category, before the test set freezes; template rules
   audited in the same session. Gold error rate reported with its interval.
2. Claim labels: confirm about 150 labelled claims (including planted negatives) for checker and judge
   calibration.
3. Blind grading: about 100 answers, mixed across systems with identity hidden and order randomised,
   for judge validation.
4. Tier-2 checks, only if those items are built, each pre-filled by me: the ~20 annotated tables for
   parser selection (confirm cell values), gold answers for the fourth manual and the agent tasks,
   counterfactual edits (confirm by eye), generated diagrams.

The 4–6 hour estimate covers sessions 1–3; tier-2 sessions are extra and optional. If a session is
skipped, the README reports the smaller check.

## Process

1. **Spike: coordinates and highlights.** 10 pages per manual through the parser candidates, boxes
   overlaid on rendered pages as screenshots, committed as reference images; includes the letter-spaced
   scan. Settles OCR handling and the coordinate pipeline before anything else.
2. **Thin end-to-end slice:** one parser, Qdrant text retrieval, page images to Qwen3.8, the claim
   format, the three-layer support check, and the highlight overlay UI, on the current 42 items.
3. **Item pool and test freeze:** write the pool, Alexander's gold-answer session, split by section,
   freeze test, choose the fourth manual.
4. **Component selection** slot by slot with the cheap checks; decision records in
   `docs/decisions/NNN-<slot>.md`.
5. **Table store; claim-label session; checker calibration; abstention operating point.**
6. **Step C** (2-day time box for one page-image retriever; no benchmark-based selection inside the box).
7. **Generators, long-context and answerable-without-reading rows; counterfactual edits; agent tasks.**
8. **Pre-registration commit; final test, held-out-manual and benchmark runs; blind-grading session.**
9. **Demo stage; README (GIF, one table, misses) and `EVAL.md`.**

Work is sequential (one builder); commits are small and described; Alexander reviews the commit list
before each push. Deferred unless time allows: DSPy (dev only), PageIndex-style navigation,
generated diagrams, a multi-turn eval beyond a handful of conversations.

## Delivery tiers

Review 3 estimated the full design at about 40–55 builder-days (2–3 calendar months with human
sessions and overnight Sparks runs). The build therefore delivers a **minimum credible version (MCV)**
first and adds the rest in a fixed order, so a cut never lands on what matters most.

**Tier 1: minimum credible version.**

- v1 re-run and re-graded with the new judge, plus a v1-at-temperature-0 row.
- Coordinate spike with 2 parsers, forced OCR, one coordinate system.
- Step D on one tuned stack: one upgraded embedder and reranker chosen on dev recall; SQLite table store
  with `lookup_table`; page images to Qwen3.8; claim format with quote match and deterministic fact
  checks; abstain when no claim survives.
- Overlay UI with verified and removed states, multi-turn chat, streaming, replay mode.
- Eval: dev ≥ 80 and a hand-written test set of 150–200 split by section; near-miss unanswerables;
  gold-answer session; independent local judge with key-fact checklists; pre-registration tag; McNemar
  and clustered bootstrap; one blind-grading session; raw outputs committed; failure taxonomy; misses.
- README (GIF, one table, misses) and `EVAL.md`.

**Tier 2: added in this order.**

1. Per-item results browser.
2. Entailment layer and checker evaluation on labelled claims.
3. Ablations: table store, page images, deterministic check.
4. "Answerable without reading" row.
5. Agent loop with 20 tasks (the brief's Phase 3).
6. Held-out fourth manual.
7. "How it found this" panel.
8. Command R7B row.
9. Open-weights configuration.
10. Long-context row.
11. Counterfactual edits (context faithfulness).
12. Step C.
13. OmniDocBench and ViDoRe in the decision records.
14. TechManualQA-700.
15. Generated diagrams.

**Cut order when time runs short** (first cut first; † means the README can no longer make that claim):
generated diagrams; TechManualQA † (unseen manuals); ViDoRe and OmniDocBench (selection rests on our
own checks); step C † (page-image retrieval at scale); counterfactual edits † (context faithfulness);
long-context row † (comparison with long context); Command R7B row † (like-for-like with v1);
open-weights configuration † (reproducible by a stranger); held-out manual † (memorisation control);
agent † (the brief's Phase 3; Alexander is asked before this cut); entailment layer † (three-layer check).

**Never cut:** the section split, pre-registration, the independent judge, the gold and blind checks,
and the misses.

## Milestone reviews of real artifacts

Independent reviews from here on look at what was built, not at this document:

- **M1, after the thin slice:** overlay reference images (including the scanned page) and 10 live
  answers on dev. Does the promise look true; are removed claims sensible?
- **M2, before the test freeze (irreversible, so the most valuable):** a sample of the item pool,
  near-misses and templates, the section split, gold-session results, judge agreement on dev, and the
  pre-registration draft with its analysis code run on dev.
- **M3, after calibration:** checker precision and recall, the real false-flag rate, and 20 removed-claim
  examples judged for product feel.
- **M4, before merge:** reproduction from a cold clone on a clean machine (replay mode and the
  open-weights path) and a two-minute read of the README by an outside reviewer.

## Presentation

The README leads with the product: a GIF of a claim lighting up its source row, then the CAGE-code
trap being caught, then one table (the pre-registered headline with its interval, one line of
method, a link to `EVAL.md`), then the misses. Decision records are summarised as one table (tried,
won, margin), highlighting the decisions that moved the number. Statistical detail, benchmarks and
counterfactual edits live in `EVAL.md` and the decision records, not the README. The long-context
router is mentioned only if it is built because the long-context row won somewhere.

## Infrastructure and the Sparks

- **Laptop WSL (x86):** parsers and OCR, embedder, reranker, entailment checker, page-image retriever
  (5090 if it needs more memory), Qdrant, SQLite, harness. ARM64 builds on the GB10 are not needed.
- **Sparks:** only the production Qwen3.8 through the existing LiteLLM gateway. No new services and no
  model swaps for retrieval work. Eval runs are rate-limited and long sweeps run at night, so
  OpenWebUI and voice latency do not suffer.
- **Judge from another family:** chosen among models that run on the laptop (or the 5090), so dev
  iterations never need the Sparks. Only if no local candidate agrees well enough with Alexander's
  grades does a Sparks judge come in, in two windows Alexander picks (dev calibration, final runs),
  each ending with Qwen3.8 restored and `smoke-test.sh`.
- **Notes for the Sparks** (`docs/SPARKS_NOTES.md`, recommendations only): the OpenWebUI vision router
  replaces images with minicpm-v descriptions although Qwen3.8 reads images itself; anything else
  learned.

## Licensing

The repo stays MIT. Permissive components (Apache-2.0, MIT, BSD) are used freely. When the best

## Decided

- Core promise: claims tied to verbatim quotes and overlay highlights; a three-layer support check
  (quote match, deterministic facts with column headers, entailment); failed claims removed behind a
  disclosure; false-flag budget; citation quality measured by an independent judge.
- Build order: coordinate spike, thin slice, then components; D then C (time-boxed); modular with ablations.
- Plain Python modules; no Haystack. SQLite table store with `lookup_table`. Agent folded into the
  answer loop.
- Retrieval models and parsers on the laptop; Sparks only for Qwen3.8 through LiteLLM.
- Eval: one item pool split by section; dev ≥ 80 and test 150–200+; exact match and key-fact
  checklists; primary judge from a non-generator family, runnable locally; one pre-registered binary
  headline with McNemar and clustered bootstrap; exploratory ablations; minimum detectable effects;
  human checks in short sessions. Held-out manual, context-faithfulness edits and up to three public
  benchmarks are tier-2 additions (see Delivery tiers).
- README: GIF, one table, misses; methodology in `EVAL.md`; demo with replay mode.
- No paid APIs; Command R7B kept as a text-only like-for-like row.
